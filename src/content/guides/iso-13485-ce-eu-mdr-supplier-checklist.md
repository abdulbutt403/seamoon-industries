ISO 13485 evidence and CE-marking evidence answer different questions: ISO 13485 concerns a medical-device quality management system within a defined scope, while CE marking is tied to a product's conformity with applicable EU requirements. Ask the supplier to show current documents for the relevant legal entity, manufacturing site, device, and market, then confirm the applicable conformity route for that specific instrument.

## Separate the quality system from product conformity

ISO 13485 is a quality management system standard for organizations involved in medical devices. A certificate can help a buyer understand that a quality system has been assessed, but it is not a product certificate and does not, by itself, show that every instrument complies with EU MDR requirements. Its scope and validity matter.

CE marking under Regulation (EU) 2017/745 (MDR) is a manufacturer's declaration that the device meets applicable requirements after the relevant conformity assessment. The procedure depends on the device and its classification. A notified body is involved where the applicable conformity procedure requires it; buyers should not assume that every device has the same certificate or assessment path.

First identify who is the legal manufacturer for the device and who will place it on the EU market. Private-label arrangements, contract manufacturing, and use of another company's brand can affect roles and obligations. Ask the supplier to explain the proposed arrangement in writing and get regulatory advice if your responsibilities are unclear.

## Questions about an ISO 13485 certificate

Ask for a complete, legible copy of the current certificate and verify it with the issuing certification body where possible. Compare the legal entity and site address on the certificate with the company and production location in your quotation. Check the certificate's validity dates and the stated scope. Does it cover the activities and device categories relevant to the proposed supply?

Ask whether important production steps are performed at the certified site or outsourced. If an external process or another factory is used, ask how the legal manufacturer controls the supplier and maintains traceability, acceptance, and change control. A certificate for one organization or location should not be assumed to cover a different site or subcontractor.

Request an explanation of how the manufacturer's quality system controls your product: approved specifications, supplier controls, incoming material checks, process validation where applicable, inspection, nonconformity handling, corrective action, document control, and lot traceability. You may not need every internal record, but you should agree which product-specific evidence will be available to you and how quality issues will be escalated.

## Questions about CE marking and EU MDR

Ask which entity is the legal manufacturer and what device name, model, catalogue number, and intended purpose are covered by the conformity documents. Confirm that the documents identify the exact instrument or clearly define the device family. If the same product is offered in several configurations, request a clear mapping between your product codes and the covered device references.

Request the EU Declaration of Conformity for the applicable device. Check that it identifies the manufacturer, device, applicable legislation, and conformity statement, and that the document is signed and controlled. If a notified body is involved, request the relevant certificate and confirm its scope, status, and notified-body identification using the European Commission's official NANDO listing. Do not assume a CE logo or a generic certificate image is adequate proof.

Ask the supplier to explain the device's classification and conformity assessment route, and whether notified body involvement is required for that device. A claim that “all instruments are CE certified” is too broad to evaluate without identifying products, intended purposes, and supporting documents. If a supplier cannot explain which documents apply to your SKU, pause the order until the gap is resolved.

Confirm which party is responsible for labeling, instructions for use, language requirements, registration or database obligations, and post-market activities for the target market. Some responsibilities depend on your role in the supply chain and the product details. Agree how changes to design, material, manufacturing site, labeling, or legal manufacturer will be communicated and approved.

## Check that the documents match the goods

Build a document checklist for each product family. It may include the current quality-system certificate, Declaration of Conformity, applicable notified-body certificate, product specification, labeling and instructions, material and batch records, and agreed inspection records. Which documents are appropriate depends on the product and supply arrangement; mark any required evidence in the purchase specification.

Compare document names and references against the quotation, approved sample, packaging, and shipment records. Check revision and issue dates, signatories, issuing organization, and certificate scope. Ask for a direct verification link or confirmation from the issuer when authenticity or current status is uncertain. Keep your own controlled record of what was reviewed and which product versions it covered.

Watch for common gaps: a certificate belonging to a different legal entity; a site not listed in the scope; an expired or suspended certificate; documents for a similar but different instrument; a declaration that does not identify the supplied product; or a CE mark presented without required supporting evidence. Treat these as questions to resolve, not as proof of wrongdoing.

## Supplier review checklist

- Identify the legal manufacturer, production site, importer, and your own role.
- Verify the ISO 13485 certificate issuer, validity, entity, site, and scope.
- Obtain the product-specific EU Declaration of Conformity where applicable.
- Confirm device identity, intended purpose, classification, and conformity route.
- Verify notified-body details and certificate scope when third-party assessment applies.
- Match documents to product codes, labels, revisions, and supplied goods.
- Agree change notification, quality records, complaint handling, and responsibility for market obligations.
